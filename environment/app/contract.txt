PAGE STORE RECOVERY CONTRACT

1. The job

Write the restart recovery routine of a small transactional page store:

    std::vector<pager::Action> pager::recover(const pager::Image& image);

The types are declared in api.hpp. After a crash, recover() receives the
durable state of the store and returns a list of storage actions. A virtual
storage device applies the actions in order and rejects any action this
contract does not allow.

recover() must not do I/O, start processes or keep anything between calls.
Its output may depend only on the Image it receives. recovery.cpp is built as
C++17 together with the provided driver.cpp and api.hpp, using only the
standard library. A failed build, an exception, a nonzero exit status or a
rejected action is a failure. Any sequence of actions that follows these rules
and reaches the required result is accepted.

2. Pages, cells and items

The store has 1 to 64 pages; page p is Image.pages[p]. A page holds an
unsigned 64-bit LSN and eight cells, each holding a signed 64-bit value. A
page is always written to stable storage whole and atomically.

The store sometimes reorganizes itself by exchanging the contents of two
cells (a SWAP record). To follow data as it moves, think of each cell as
holding one item. UPDATE and CLR records change the value of the item in a
cell. A SWAP exchanges the items of two cells, values and all, so after it the
item that was in one cell is in the other. Items never move in any other way.

A log record touches page p if it is an UPDATE or CLR whose page is p, or a
SWAP whose page or page2 is p. This holds even when the record leaves every
value unchanged. Applying a SWAP to page p sets just those of its two cells
that are on page p, to the values given in the record.

Image.base_lsn is a checkpoint. When it was taken no transaction was running
and every page had some contents, not necessarily zeros. Version L of page p
is what you get by starting from the checkpoint contents of page p and applying
every record that touches page p with LSN at most L, in LSN order; its page
LSN is L. This counts every record that was ever durable, including records
later removed by truncation (section 3). Only base_lsn and the LSNs of such
records that touch page p name versions of page p.

Each entry of Image.pages is some version of its page, and different pages can
be at different versions. A page may hold changes of transactions that never
committed, and may lack changes of transactions that did. Every record a page
version contains was durable before that version was written.

3. Log records

Image.log is the durable log in increasing LSN order. LSNs are greater than
base_lsn, strictly increasing and may have gaps. They can exceed INT64_MAX and
stay below UINT64_MAX - 1000000. No record has LSN 0; in a pointer field, 0
means "none".

A record has the fields lsn, kind, tx, prev, undo, page, slot, before, after,
target, page2 and slot2. Except for SWAP, every record belongs to the
transaction tx, a positive 32-bit ID that is never reused. prev is the LSN of
the previous record of the same transaction, or 0 for its first record, so
each transaction's records form a chain. A transaction begins with its first
record. Fields not listed for a kind are 0.

  1 UPDATE   Assigns `after` to the item in cell `slot` of page `page`.
             `before` is the value the cell held just before. The two may be
             equal.
  2 CLR      Compensates the UPDATE whose LSN is `target`. It assigns that
             UPDATE's before value to the item the UPDATE changed, in whatever
             cell holds that item when the CLR is written; page and slot name
             that cell. after is the UPDATE's before value, before is 0, and
             undo is the UPDATE's prev.
  3 COMMIT   The transaction has committed.
  4 ABORT    The transaction is rolling back completely.
  5 END      The transaction is finished. It writes nothing after END.
  6 PREPARE  The transaction is prepared and waits for an outside decision.
  7 NESTED   Closes a nested top action. undo is the LSN of the
             transaction's record just before the action began, or 0 if the
             action began the transaction.
  8 SWAP     Exchanges the items in cell `slot` of page `page` and cell
             `slot2` of page `page2`; the two cells are different. before is
             the value that was in the first cell and moves to the second;
             after is the value that was in the second cell and moves to the
             first. A SWAP is written by the store itself, not by a
             transaction: tx, prev, undo and target are 0. It is never undone.

A transaction's state comes from the latest of its COMMIT, ABORT, PREPARE and
END records: committed, in ABORT state, prepared or ended. With none of them it
is active. An active transaction may write UPDATE, CLR, NESTED, COMMIT, ABORT
or PREPARE. A prepared transaction may write only COMMIT or ABORT. A committed
transaction may write only END. A transaction in ABORT state may write only
CLR and, once nothing is left to roll back, END.

Rolling back compensates a transaction's UPDATEs one at a time with CLRs,
newest first. An UPDATE that already has a CLR is never compensated again,
and neither is an UPDATE inside a closed nested top action; CLRs themselves
are never compensated. The UPDATEs that remain are the transaction's rollback
work. An active transaction may roll back only part of that work and then go
on with new updates.

A nested top action is one or more UPDATEs of a transaction closed by a
NESTED record of the same transaction. Its UPDATEs are exactly the records of
that transaction after the one named by NESTED.undo (from its first record if
undo is 0) and before the NESTED record; no other kind of record of that
transaction falls in between. Once the NESTED record exists, these changes are
permanent: they are kept whatever happens to the transaction later. Updates
with no NESTED record closing them are ordinary updates.

Locking is per item. An item owned by one transaction is never changed by
another. A transaction owns each item it changes outside a nested top action
until its END, including while it is prepared and after it has partly rolled
back. A nested top action changes only items that no transaction, including
its own, owned when the action began; the transaction owns them until the
NESTED record releases them. It can change them again later and then owns them
as usual. Moving an item with a SWAP does not change who owns it.

The log may have been truncated at the front, so records up to some LSN can
be missing. Truncation never removes a record of a transaction that has no END
record, and never removes a record whose LSN is higher than the stable LSN of
a page it touches.

4. Outside decisions

Image.decisions maps a transaction ID to 1 (commit) or 2 (abort). Every
transaction listed there has a durable PREPARE record, which truncation may
since have removed along with the rest of the transaction. When recovery runs,
the transaction may still be prepared, or the decision may already have been
carried out, so that it has committed, is rolling back or has ended. A
prepared transaction with no decision stays prepared.

5. Required result

When recover() returns and the device has applied all of its actions:

  - A transaction is a winner if it has a COMMIT record or is prepared with a
    commit decision. Every other transaction, except a prepared one with no
    decision, is a loser.
  - Every winner and every loser has an END record. Losers have been rolled
    back completely, so none of their rollback work is left.
  - Prepared transactions with no decision get no new records and are still
    prepared, with their changes in place.
  - Every log record recover() appended is durable.
  - Every stable page contains every log record that touches it: its LSN is
    at least the LSN of each of those records.
  - Calling recover() again on the resulting state would append no records.
    Rewriting a page with identical contents is fine.

6. Storage actions

An Action's op is 'A', 'F' or 'P'. Fields the op does not use are ignored.

A  Append Action.record to the log buffer in memory. Its lsn must be one more
   than the LSN of the last record in the log, durable or buffered. Its prev
   must be the LSN of its transaction's latest record. The transaction must
   already appear in the log and must not have ended. Recovery may append only:
     ABORT   for an active transaction, or for a prepared one with an abort
             decision;
     COMMIT  for a prepared transaction with a commit decision;
     CLR     for a transaction in ABORT state with rollback work left; it must
             compensate the newest UPDATE of that work, with the fields
             described in section 3;
     END     for a committed transaction, or for one in ABORT state with no
             rollback work left.
   All fields not described for the kind, including page2 and slot2, are 0.

F  Make the log durable through Action.upto. upto must be the LSN of a record
   in the log at or after the last durable record. That record and everything
   before it become durable; later records stay in the buffer. F writes no
   pages.

P  Write Action.page to stable storage as page Action.page_id. The page must
   exist, and Action.page must be exactly a version of that page (section 2).
   Its LSN may not be lower than the LSN of the page now in stable storage, and
   may not be higher than the LSN of the last durable log record. Rewriting
   the version that is already in stable storage is always allowed.

7. Crashes

The device can crash after any prefix of the returned actions, including the
empty prefix and the complete list. A crash loses buffered log records and
keeps durable records and finished page writes. recover() is then called in a
fresh process with what survived and the same decisions. This can repeat any
number of times before a call is allowed to finish. The Image may also come
from a crash during an earlier recovery attempt made by different software; all
you can rely on is that every earlier action followed this contract.

A watchdog may also stop any attempt once its first 16 actions have taken
effect, which is the same as a crash at that point. Recovery must finish even
if this happens to every attempt. So whenever work is left (a transaction
other than an undecided prepared one has no END record, or a stable page's LSN
is below that of a log record that touches it), the first 16 actions recover()
returns must include an F that makes at least one more record durable or a P
that raises the LSN of a stable page.

There are no torn pages, no media errors and no concurrent foreground work.

8. Limits

At most 64 pages and 128 transaction IDs. A history written before the first
recovery attempt has at most 4096 records; with records added by interrupted
recovery attempts, the log can hold up to 32768. The log may be empty, and
there may be nothing to do.

The driver rejects more than 12 * (input log records) + 8 * 128 + 2 * (pages)
actions. Each recover() call has 10 seconds of CPU time, 512 MiB of address
space and 16 MiB of output. The build has 60 seconds.

9. Driver format

driver.cpp reads decimal numbers separated by whitespace:

  base_lsn page_count record_count decision_count
  for each page:      page_lsn cell0 ... cell7
  for each record:    lsn kind tx prev undo page slot before after target
                      page2 slot2
  for each decision:  tx decision

and prints one action per line, with no count first:

  A lsn kind tx prev undo page slot before after target page2 slot2
  F upto
  P page_id page_lsn cell0 ... cell7

Implement recover(); do not write a second main() or replace the driver.
